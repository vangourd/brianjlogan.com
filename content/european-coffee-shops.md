I didn't realize how spoiled Americans are with coffee access until I spent two weeks in Europe.

In America, you're one exit away from a 7brew, Dutch Bros, Starbucks, probably a local coffee shop. Any town, any highway, there's coffee. Third wave coffee is everywhere now. I took that for granted.

Maybe I didn't go to the right areas. Maybe I was just a blind tourist with no idea about the customs. But I couldn't find coffee shops like we have in the States. Europe has cafes where you order espresso at a bar or sit for an hour, and the coffee they do have is mostly second wave French roast—dark, bitter, over-roasted.

American coffee shops are full of business people, young teenagers, moms with kids. Energetic conversations happening, kids playing with toys in the corner. It's this vibrant community space.

People criticize it for being too expensive and tell you to buck up and make coffee at home. But it's a wonderful aspect of American life. The accessibility, the variety, the light roasts, the atmosphere—I missed all of it.
